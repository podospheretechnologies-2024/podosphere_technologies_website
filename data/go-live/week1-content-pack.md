We built an AI that can draft a week of social posts in about 90 seconds.

1. Podo AI drafts the calendar, captions, replies and ad suggestions.
2. Everything lands in one approval queue.
3. You approve with one click. Or edit. Or reject.

### M3 · X thread · 12:00 · Agency Playbook · MANUAL
1/ 5 SOPs every social media agency needs before client number four. Without them you scale chaos.

2/ Onboarding SOP. Brand kit, logins, approver, posting windows, "never say" list. One form, one day. If onboarding takes two weeks, the client is already judging you.

3/ Approval SOP. One queue, one approver per client, 24-hour SLA. No approvals in WhatsApp screenshots.

4/ Reporting SOP. Same template every month, numbers the client can read in two minutes, one "what we will change" line. Reports that need a call to explain are not reports.

5/ Inbox SOP. Who answers, how fast, which replies are pre-approved. Leads get flagged, not buried.

6/ Repurposing SOP. One hero asset a week becomes clips, a carousel, two text posts and a story. Ideas are the bottleneck, not output.

7/ We built Podo Social so an agency can run all five from one workspace, with a human approving every post. Running our own channels on it now, in public.

The case against fully autonomous marketing, in four lines.

1. A model does not know which client is nervous this week.
2. A model does not know the ad budget just got cut on a call.
3. A model does not carry the reputation when a post lands badly.
4. A model is brilliant at the draft and blind to the context.

So we split the job.

Podo AI drafts the posts, labels the inbox, scores the leads and flags the fatigued ad set. A human approves each one in a single queue, in about ten minutes a day.

80% of the grind gone. 100% of the judgment kept.

That is not a limitation we apologise for. It is the product.

If you run an agency, where would you draw that line?

### F3 · X thread · 12:00 · Agency Playbook · MANUAL
1/ How to price a social media retainer without underselling yourself. A thread for agency owners.

2/ Price the outcome and the cadence, not the hours. "12 posts, 4 Reels, daily inbox, monthly report" is a product. "40 hours" is a timesheet.

3/ Three tiers. Starter: publishing plus report. Growth: plus inbox and leads. Pro: plus ads monitoring. Most clients pick the middle one if the middle one exists.

4/ Put the approval SLA in the contract. 24 hours to approve or it rolls to next week. Protects your calendar and their results.

5/ Charge for onboarding. One day of real work deserves a line item. It also filters the clients who will not show up.

6/ Review pricing every six months with your own numbers: hours per client, inbox volume, revisions. Tools that cut the drafting hours change the maths. That is the whole reason we built one.
