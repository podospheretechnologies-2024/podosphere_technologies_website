# Ship secure client links before portals

All ten competitors model an agency the same basic way: **one agency tenant that holds many hard-walled client containers**. The containers have different names: a GoHighLevel Location, a Zoho Brand, an Agorapulse workspace, a Vista profile group, a Sprout Group, a Sendible or Planable workspace, an AiSensy Business/Project, and in Interakt a separate account per business. Staff get access to each client in one of two ways. Either they hold an agency-wide role, or they are explicitly assigned to that client and given a per-client role or permission set on top. Podo Social's current schema (`Organization` → `SocialCustomer`, with `SocialClientMember.permissions` as JSON and `ClientApprover` keyed by customer and email) already matches this shape. The competitors differ in three places, and that is where Podo can win. First, approval links: every serious tool lets clients approve without a login or a paid seat, but **only Vista Social publicly documents password and expiry controls**, and nobody documents single-client scoping, rotation per approver, or an audit trail. Second, onboarding: Sendible and SocialPilot let clients connect their own accounts through OAuth links, while most others gate that to top tiers. Third, pricing: the agency-friendly pattern is to **charge per client container with unlimited or free client logins** (Sendible, Planable, Zoho), not per seat (Agorapulse, Sprout). For an Indian agency market, SocialPilot's INR price points (₹1,700–₹6,000/month, with client features only on Premium) are the nearest benchmark. Podo's real opening is a combination nobody offers publicly: social publishing, Meta ads and WhatsApp in one client container, plus an all-clients approvals and calendar view, which no vendor documents.

## Every vendor walls clients into a container, and gates that wall to higher tiers

GoHighLevel is the strictest and clearest model. It has two levels, the agency ("Company") and sub-accounts ("Locations"). Users are either **Agency type, scoped to everything, or Account type, who "can access only the sub-accounts assigned to them"**. Each user is then an Admin or User with module and granular permissions on top ([HighLevel Help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000002543-manage-agency-user-roles-and-permissions-in-highlevel)). HighLevel explicitly warns that adding clients at agency level "will give them unnecessary visibility across all sub-accounts". Sub-account Admins always inherit full access within their sub-account, so the way to limit a client is a User role ([HighLevel Help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001078296)). Impersonation ("Login As") is **enabled by default** and can be disabled per admin ([HighLevel Help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000002543-manage-agency-user-roles-and-permissions-in-highlevel)). Zoho Social uses Portal → Brand. Its pricing page lists Agency at **10 clients** and Agency Plus at **20**, each with 5-client add-ons and 5 team members. Clients get free logins to a portal that shows only their brand ([Zoho pricing](https://www.zoho.com/social/pricing.html)). Zoho states the reason plainly: the agency dashboard "contains other businesses' data as well" ([Zoho community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en-gb/community/topic/social-media-simplified-with-zoho-social-customize-your-portal-for-your-clients?page=2)).

The social-first tools converge on the same shape but sell isolation as a premium feature. Agorapulse runs **Organization → Workspaces → Profiles**, with one organization role plus a separate role on each profile (Admin, Editor, Moderator, Guest). However, "workspace access management", which restricts a Member to particular workspaces, is **Custom-plan only**, and Co-owners and Managers always see every workspace ([Agorapulse](https://support.agorapulse.com/en/articles/9542481-team-settings-explained)). Vista Social recommends one profile group per client. It offers a **Profile Group Admin** role with full control over only that group, plus Restricted Users with No Access / View / Manage per feature and a "Contributor" publish level whose posts go to Pending Approval ([Vista help](https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409604728219)). Group settings can cap profiles and users per group ([Vista help](https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/21958238548123)). Sprout organises access by Groups. Each user gets a per-profile level, **No Access, Read Only, Needs Approval, Can Reply or Full Publishing**, with feature permissions held separately ([Sprout guide](https://media.sproutsocial.com/uploads/Sprout-Social-Message-Approval-Product-Guide.pdf)). Sprout allows a profile to sit in several groups, which makes isolation weaker than a one-profile-one-client rule.

Planable has the cleanest data model of the set. **Client is a membership type, separate from role.** Client members see only external-facing content and comments, while team members also see internal notes and drafts. On top of that sit a role (Owner, Admin, Contributor, Writer, Approver, Guest) and toggles (View, Approve, Edit, Publish, Analyze, Engage, Admin), and one person can hold different roles in different workspaces ([Planable guide](https://planable.io/guides/planable-workspaces/)). Sendible and SocialPilot treat the client as a user type. In Sendible, a Client reaches only its own dashboard unless granted "Also has access to", and **every Admin sees all client dashboards** ([Sendible support](https://support.sendible.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014576251), snippet). In SocialPilot, a Client role can never later be converted to a team role ([SocialPilot help](https://help.socialpilot.co/article/573-how-do-i-add-clients-to-my-team)). The WhatsApp vendors use a different structure: a separate reseller console sits above per-client tenants. AiSensy partners create each client as a "Business" in partner.aisensy.com, which automatically gets a default "Project" ([AiSensy partner doc](https://partnership-support-doc.helpscoutdocs.com/article/29-partner-dashboard-quick-guide), snippet; page now 404). Interakt's "Unified Partner Portal" tracks leads and earnings rather than operating client accounts ([Interakt](https://interakt.shop/partner-program)). **No vendor publicly documents a unified cross-client inbox or calendar.** The documented roll-ups are agency dashboards and scheduled reports, such as GHL's agency-level Report Scheduler, which is gated to the $497 plan ([HighLevel Help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001236324-setting-up-automated-email-reports-for-sub-accounts-with-the-report-scheduler)).

## No-login approval is table stakes, but link security is almost undocumented

The magic-link approver Podo already plans is mainstream. GoHighLevel's Social Planner shares posts through "**secure magic links**" with no login. Approvers can approve or reject with notes, reschedule, and take bulk actions, and one link can cover many posts and be sent over email, WhatsApp or Slack ([HighLevel changelog](https://ideas.gohighlevel.com/changelog/social-planner-share-approve-posts-via-magic-links)). Agorapulse's Shared Calendar lets external reviewers approve, reject and comment from an email invite or link, with no account ([Agorapulse](https://support.agorapulse.com/en/articles/12010367-how-to-access-the-shared-calendar-as-an-external-user), snippet). Plan caps apply: 5 calendars on Advanced and unlimited on Custom ([Agorapulse pricing](https://www.agorapulse.com/pricing/)). SocialPilot's "Approvals on the Go" lets clients "Approve to Publish" or "Send back" with comments "without the need to log in" ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/product-update/approvals-on-the-go)). Sprout puts **email addresses of non-Sprout users** directly into workflow steps ([Sprout help](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/205974715), snippet). The holdouts are Zoho, where competitors claim a client "has to be invited and log in to review a post" ([Cloud Campaign](https://www.cloudcampaign.com/lp/vs-zoho-social), competitor claim), and Sendible, which documents no no-login approval at all. Planable's public link allows only **viewing and commenting**, and approval through it is unconfirmed ([Planable help](https://help.planable.io/en/articles/3803135-share-posts-with-a-public-link), snippet).

Security is where the field is thin. **Vista Social is the only vendor with documented link controls.** Its links support an optional date range, an **expiry date, a password**, and auto-delete on expiry that permanently removes the link and its approval history access. Vista also advises hiding "in review" posts until it is the client's turn ([Vista help](https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/18158890067355-Shared-Calendar-Reviewing-content-without-logging-in), snippet). Vista also makes the client a **typed step inside a multi-step workflow**: internal steps 1–2 come first, and the shared-calendar client step comes last ([Vista blog](https://vistasocial.com/insights/social-media-post-approval-workflow-in-vista-social/)). Planable documents a similar internal-then-client chain, but only on Enterprise ([Planable guide](https://planable.io/guides/planable-workspaces/)). Agorapulse reserves multi-step approval for Custom ([Agorapulse pricing](https://www.agorapulse.com/pricing/)). The only other documented link controls apply to *invite* links. SocialPilot's can be disabled or refreshed, which invalidates the old link ([SocialPilot help](https://help.socialpilot.co/article/573-how-do-i-add-clients-to-my-team)). Planable's **lapse after 7 days** and can be deleted ([Planable guide](https://planable.io/guides/planable-workspaces/)). On report links, Sprout offers Public or Invite-only sharing with optional expiry ([Sprout help](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/24821431669773), snippet). No vendor publicly documents token lifetime, per-approver tokens, single-use behaviour, revocation of an individual approver, or an audit of which link approved which post.

Two workflow ideas are worth copying directly. SocialPilot's **Auto-Approve publishes posts still pending one hour before schedule** ([SocialPilot help](https://help.socialpilot.co/article/343-how-do-i-manage-my-clients-access)). This is practical for slow-responding clients, but it must be an explicit per-client opt-in. Planable adds per-workspace reminders for pending approvals and can lock content after approval ([Planable guide](https://planable.io/guides/planable-workspaces/)).

## Self-connect onboarding splits "connect accounts" from "approve content"

Sendible's Client Connect is a widget the agency embeds on its own website. Clients **authorise their social accounts via OAuth, so no passwords change hands** ([Sendible](https://sendible.com/insights/client-connect-a-safe-way-to-access-clients-social-media-accounts)). When a token lapses, Sendible **emails the client a reconnection link that works without a dashboard login** ([Sendible](https://www.sendible.com/features/client-connect?hsLang=en)), and it includes this on every tier ([Sendible pricing](https://www.sendible.com/pricing)). SocialPilot's reusable Client Invite Link works for multiple clients, can be **restricted to specific platforms**, and can be disabled or refreshed. Clients cannot disconnect accounts themselves ([SocialPilot help](https://help.socialpilot.co/article/573-how-do-i-add-clients-to-my-team)). Agorapulse offers a free-seat **Profile Connector** role that can connect, renew and remove only the profiles it connected, and cannot publish or view reports ([Agorapulse](https://support.agorapulse.com/en/articles/9542481-team-settings-explained)). Vista gates "client profile connect" to Scale at $449/month ([Vista pricing](https://vistasocial.com/pricing/)). On WhatsApp, AiSensy and Interakt onboard client numbers through Meta **Embedded Signup**. Interakt's Tech Provider page says Tech Providers "do not have the right to... charg[e] any markup on conversation fees", but its own FAQ contradicts this ([Interakt blog](https://www.interakt.shop/blog/become-a-whatsapp-business-partner), 2024). Interakt bills conversation charges from a **separate prepaid wallet per account** ([Interakt FAQ](https://www.interakt.shop/resource-center/prepaid-wallet-faqs-for-enterprise-customers/), snippet).

GoHighLevel snapshots show where a "clone this client's setup" feature must stop. They copy workflows, funnels, calendars, forms, templates and custom fields. They **exclude contacts, conversations, Stripe connections and integrations** ([HighLevel Help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48000982512-creating-new-snapshots)). They also offer an "Assets Protected" mode that blocks re-sharing ([HighLevel Help](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000002852-assets-protected-snapshots-called-ip-protected-earlier-)).

## Reports and white-label are the main upsell, priced per client rather than per seat

White-label is consistently the top-tier lever, except that Agorapulse includes white-label profile reports on every plan. Agorapulse saves scheduled emails and PPTX for Advanced, and its Reports Open API is Custom-only ([Agorapulse pricing](https://www.agorapulse.com/pricing/)). Vista puts white-label reports and dashboard on Scale. It sells a **Custom Domain Whitelabeling** add-on that covers shared calendars, report links, connect links and notification emails ([Vista help](https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/16852036868507), snippet). Zoho Agency includes agency-branded scheduled reports and a client portal on `yourcompany.social-public.com` or a custom domain, which Zoho takes "5-6 business days" to verify ([Zoho community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en-gb/community/topic/social-media-simplified-with-zoho-social-customize-your-portal-for-your-clients?page=2)). Sendible's white-label covers a custom domain and branded approval emails, but it is a paid add-on on Elite and Enterprise only ([Sendible pricing](https://www.sendible.com/pricing)). SocialPilot white-labels the logo, UI and PDF reports, which recipients open without logging in ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/features/client-management)). Sprout limits scheduled PDF delivery to Advanced ([Sprout help](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002389306-Scheduling-and-Sending-Report-PDFs), snippet) and has no documented white-label. AiSensy's white-label is a full rebrand: domain, logo, pricing, and embedded signup without AiSensy branding. Partners set **their own margin on subscriptions plus a markup on conversation charges** ([AiSensy](https://m.aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-api-whitelabelled-partnership/)).

The pricing unit splits the market. Sendible charges by workspace: **Core 1 workspace/6 profiles up to Enterprise 50/300, unlimited users on every tier** ([Sendible pricing](https://www.sendible.com/pricing)). Planable charges **$33 or $49 per workspace per month with unlimited users**, and multi-level approval needs Enterprise ([Planable pricing](https://planable.io/pricing/)). Zoho caps team seats at 5 but makes **client logins free** ([Zoho pricing](https://www.zoho.com/social/pricing.html)). Agorapulse charges per user, from $79 to $199 ([Agorapulse pricing](https://www.agorapulse.com/pricing/)). Sprout charges per seat, from $79 to $399 ([Sprout pricing](https://sproutsocial.com/pricing/)). Both punish large agency teams. SocialPilot's India-served page lists **Standard ₹1,700–2,000/month (10 accounts, 1 user, no client management)** and **Premium ₹5,100–6,000/month (30 accounts, 3 users, client management, white-label reports, client approval)**, with extra accounts and users at ₹200/month ([SocialPilot pricing](https://www.socialpilot.co/pricing)). SocialPilot clients with Publish permission consume a team seat ([SocialPilot help](https://help.socialpilot.co/article/573-how-do-i-add-clients-to-my-team)). Treat USD figures from third parties, including GHL's $297 and $497 tiers and Zoho's $320 Agency plan, as unverified.

Public APIs follow the container exactly. GHL issues OAuth tokens with `userType` set to `"Company"` or `"Location"`, and exchanges an agency token for a location token via `POST /oauth/locationToken` with `companyId` and `locationId`. Refresh tokens are single-use and rotating ([HighLevel OAuth](https://marketplace.gohighlevel.com/docs/Authorization/OAuth2.0)). Its Private Integration Tokens are scoped at creation, with **90-day rotation and a 7-day overlap** recommended ([HighLevel PIT](https://marketplace.gohighlevel.com/docs/Authorization/PrivateIntegrationsToken)). Sprout puts every path under `/v1/{customer_id}/…`, discovers accessible customers through `/v1/metadata/client`, limits publishing to profiles within a single `group_id`, and rate-limits at 60 requests/min ([Sprout API](https://api.sproutsocial.com/docs/)). Agorapulse's read-only analytics API requires **organization ID + workspace ID + profile ID on every request**, and its personal keys mirror the user's permissions ([Agorapulse](https://support.agorapulse.com/en/articles/9155447-analytics-open-api), snippet). Vista exposes profile groups as the scoping unit in its MCP tools ([Vista MCP](https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/46525029430299-Vista-Social-s-Available-MCP-Tools)).

## What Podo Social should copy, and where it should go further

**Copy the two-axis access model, and make the client side explicit.** Keep `Organization` membership roles (owner, admin, member) as the agency axis. Use `SocialClientMember` as the GHL "Account user" assignment, and add an org-level "all clients" flag for staff who see everything, as Agorapulse Managers and Sendible Admins do. Borrow Planable's `membership_type ∈ {team, client}` instead of overloading a role. Client members should never see internal notes or drafts, and per GHL's warning, a client must never hold an Organization membership. The current `permissions Json` should become a typed set of toggles modelled on SocialPilot and Planable: view, comment, approve, publish, connect accounts, analytics, inbox, ads view, WhatsApp. Add a Sprout-style "Needs Approval" publishing level for junior staff. Make `customerId` a mandatory filter in every repository query, the way Sprout and Agorapulse require the container ID on every API call. Keep the rule that one integration belongs to exactly one SocialCustomer, which is stricter than Sprout's many-to-many groups.

**Beat everyone on approval-link security, because nobody documents it.** Issue magic links per `ClientApprover`, not per calendar, so each approval is attributable to one person and one client. Store only a hash of the token. Scope each token to one `customerId`. Set a default expiry, for example 7 days as Planable does for invites, and add Vista's optional password and date range. Support per-approver revocation and SocialPilot-style rotation, record IP, user-agent and timestamp on every decision, and show the audit trail in the post history. Follow Vista on the workflow side: the client should be a typed final step after internal review, hidden until it is their turn. Offer SocialPilot's T-1h auto-approve and Planable's reminders as per-client opt-ins. WhatsApp delivery of approval links, which GHL supports, is the obvious India-first channel. Keep **connect and reconnect links separate from approve links**, as Sendible does, so a leaked approval link cannot attach or detach accounts. Give the connect link SocialPilot's platform allow-list, and email or WhatsApp a reconnect link when a Meta token lapses.

**Price per client, not per seat, and use the combined view as the differentiator.** Sendible, Planable and Zoho show that agencies prefer unlimited or free client logins with a cap on client containers. Set Podo's plan limits as clients × channels per client, with Vista-style per-client caps, and count client approvers as free. Benchmark the agency tier against SocialPilot Premium's ₹5,100–6,000. Hold white-label to a paid tier, starting with branded PDF reports and an approval page on the agency's own domain. Zoho's 5–6 day domain verification and Vista's SSL handling show that this is real operational work. For WhatsApp, follow Interakt's per-account wallet pattern with a per-SocialCustomer credit ledger. Do not plan to mark up Meta conversation fees unless Podo goes through a BSP arrangement, given the Tech Provider restriction Interakt describes. A cross-client approvals queue and calendar, which no competitor documents, is the most defensible feature to build next. Build it on the same assignment table, so staff see only the clients they are assigned to.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which hierarchy?" to "which trust boundary?". The hierarchy is settled: Organization → SocialCustomer is the industry model, and Podo's schema already has it. The open problem is the trust boundary at the client edge: links, tokens and portal logins that reach outside the agency. The incumbents treat this edge casually. Most document no expiry, no per-person tokens and no audit, and some sell isolation itself as an enterprise upsell. A tool that ships per-approver, revocable, expiring, audited links together with strict client scoping on every query would be *more* secure at the entry tier than Agorapulse or Planable are at their top tiers. That is a credible sales argument to Indian agencies handling client brand accounts and ad spend.

Some uncertainty remains. Several conclusions rest on help-centre snippets, because Sprout, Vista and Sendible pages returned 403 to direct fetches. Prices conflict across sources, and no product UIs were inspected directly. A combined all-clients view may exist inside GHL or Agorapulse without being documented. Before Podo finalises pricing and positions the cross-client view as unique, it should verify both in live trials, and confirm the SocialPilot INR prices, which were geo-localised.
